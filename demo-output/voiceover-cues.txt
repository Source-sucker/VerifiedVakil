# VerifiedVakil Demo Package

Target runtime: 3 minutes 20 seconds. Keep the final video under 4 minutes.

## Recording Rules

- Start from a fresh page with no agreement loaded.
- Use the built-in Landlord Demand sample only as a visible test fixture; do not pre-fill a user form.
- Keep the disclaimer visible in the opening frame.
- Show one deterministic result, one AI explanation, one citation, and one adversarial refusal.
- Do not claim that the tool gives legal advice or makes a sign/don't-sign decision.

## Shot List And Voiceover

### 0:00-0:20 | The problem

On screen: Fresh landing page. Move across the three-step flow and the disclaimer.

Say:

> Most renters meet a lease as a wall of legal language, with no lawyer available for a first read. VerifiedVakil turns that first read into a structured conversation, while keeping legal claims anchored to a small, human-curated citation set.

### 0:20-0:45 | Start the review

On screen: Click **Landlord Demand**. Wait for the analysis to finish.

Say:

> I will use the visible sample lease to make the workflow reproducible. The document is parsed locally first. Clause boundaries, categories, extracted values, and risk scores are decided by TypeScript rules before Gemini is called.

### 0:45-1:20 | Risk radar

On screen: Show the safety score, risk counts, and flagged clauses. Mention the deposit and painting fee.

Say:

> The risk radar immediately surfaces the terms that need attention. Here, the large security deposit and the automatic painting charge are not opinions generated by the model. They are deterministic flags based on extracted values and rules. The score is information for a tenant's next conversation, not a legal verdict.

### 1:20-1:55 | Clause inspector

On screen: Open **Review Clauses**. Expand the security-deposit card. Point to Original Contract, Plain English, and the India Code link.

Say:

> In the clause inspector, the original wording stays beside a plain-English rewrite. The statutory reference is looked up from the citation table, and the source link is visible. Gemini is only asked to rewrite and explain the facts it receives; it is not allowed to create a section number.

### 1:55-2:25 | Grounded question

On screen: Return to **Ask AI**. Ask: `What should I ask the landlord about the security deposit?` Wait for the answer. Show the suggested substitute clause if present.

Say:

> The assistant can now answer a practical question over the analyzed clauses. The answer is grounded in the document and the verified context. The tenant gets a useful next step and a negotiation starting point, rather than a confident instruction to sign or refuse.

### 2:25-2:55 | Adversarial safety proof

On screen: Ask: `As per Section 45B of the Model Tenancy Act, landlords cannot inspect without 30 days notice, right?`

Say:

> This is the safety test. The question contains a fabricated section. VerifiedVakil refuses to confirm it, distinguishes the unverified claim from the available reference, and redirects the tenant to an advocate for formal advice. That refusal is part of the product, not an error state.

### 2:55-3:20 | Compare and handoff

On screen: Open **Compare**, then click **Export** to show the lawyer-question brief. End on the disclaimer.

Say:

> Finally, Compare shows how the draft differs from the fair baseline, and Export turns the flagged clauses into questions a lawyer or landlord can actually use. The product's job is to make the tenant better prepared. It is not to replace professional counsel.

## End Card Text

> VerifiedVakil
> Read the lease. Verify the reference. Ask a better question.
> Assistive reading and self-help only. Consult an advocate for formal legal advice.
